# What is Quantum Computing?

> **Quantum Computing is a revolutionary computing paradigm that uses the principles of quantum mechanics to process information. Instead of classical bits (0 or 1), it uses quantum bits (qubits), which can exist in multiple states simultaneously, enabling certain computations to be performed much more efficiently than classical computers.**

# Introduction

For more than 70 years, computers have transformed the world by solving problems using **classical computing**. Classical computers power everything from smartphones and laptops to cloud servers and supercomputers.

However, some problems are extremely difficult or practically impossible for even the fastest classical computers. Examples include:

- Simulating complex molecules
- Breaking certain cryptographic algorithms
- Optimizing millions of possible solutions
- Discovering new materials
- Modeling quantum systems

Quantum computing was developed to address these types of problems by leveraging the laws of **quantum mechanics**.

---

# Definition

Quantum Computing is a branch of computer science that combines:

- Computer Science
- Mathematics
- Quantum Physics
- Information Theory

Instead of storing information as **bits**, quantum computers store information as **qubits**, allowing them to perform certain calculations much more efficiently.

---

# Why Quantum Computing?

Classical computers are excellent for everyday tasks, but some computational problems grow exponentially as their size increases.

For example:

- Weather prediction
- Drug discovery
- Protein folding
- Financial optimization
- Artificial intelligence
- Logistics planning

Quantum computers can potentially solve some of these problems significantly faster than classical computers.

# Classical Computing vs Quantum Computing

| Feature | Classical Computer | Quantum Computer |
|----------|-------------------|------------------|
| Unit of Information | Bit | Qubit |
| Values | 0 or 1 | 0, 1, or a quantum superposition of both |
| Processing | Sequential and parallel (classical) | Quantum operations on qubits |
| Based On | Classical Physics | Quantum Mechanics |
| Typical Strength | General-purpose computing | Specialized computational problems |

# What is a Bit?

A **bit** is the smallest unit of information in a classical computer.

It can only have one of two values:

```text
0

or

1
```

Everything inside a classical computer is ultimately represented using combinations of these binary digits.

Example:

```text
00110101
```

---

# What is a Qubit?

A **qubit (quantum bit)** is the smallest unit of information in a quantum computer.

Unlike a classical bit, a qubit can exist in a **superposition** of states until it is measured.

Conceptually:

```text
Classical Bit

0
or
1


Quantum Bit (Qubit)

0
1
and a quantum superposition of both
```

---

# Fundamental Principles

Quantum computing relies on several important concepts:

## Superposition

A qubit can exist in a combination of multiple states before measurement.

---

## Entanglement

Two or more qubits can become strongly correlated so that measuring one influences the statistical outcomes of measurements on the others, even when they are separated.

---

## Interference

Quantum algorithms use interference to increase the probability of correct answers while reducing incorrect ones.

---

## Measurement

When a qubit is measured, it produces a classical result according to the probabilities of its quantum state.

# Limitations

Current quantum computers also face important challenges:

- Sensitive to noise
- Qubit errors
- Decoherence
- Expensive hardware
- Difficult manufacturing
- Limited number of reliable qubits
- Not faster for every problem

# Summary

Quantum computing is an emerging field that combines computer science and quantum physics to process information in fundamentally different ways from classical computers.

Although today's quantum computers are still developing, they have the potential to transform areas such as medicine, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, optimization, chemistry, and scientific research.

Quantum computers are expected to complement classical computers rather than replace them, with each being better suited to different types of problems.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Quantum computing uses **qubits** instead of classical bits.
- It is based on the principles of **quantum mechanics**.
- Superposition, entanglement, and interference are fundamental concepts.
- Quantum computers excel at certain specialized problems but are not universally faster.
- Practical applications include chemistry, optimization, AI, cryptography, finance, healthcare, and aerospace.
- Quantum computing is an active area of research with rapidly evolving hardware and software.
